Without quite noticing, I have already turned 32.

The midlife crisis is drawing closer. In the next three years, I have to find a path, or things will really get bad.

First, the main quest is to hang on: stay at this company for at least two more years. Next year I will mark my fifth anniversary, so I can collect the long-service award first. If I get the chance, I will stay on until 35. By then N will be 7 or 8, and even if I do get laid off, I will have some room to get back on my feet.

Second, I have to force myself to carry out the side quest. I will not think about anything else. I will stick to one path: building sites for overseas audiences. Too many ideas usually means nothing gets done. This year I started strong and fizzled out. In the first half I was full of resolve; by August I had lost steam, and the last three months have basically been a state of giving up. Next month I will go to the year-end gathering in Shenzhen to get a boost, and I will also stop by Hong Kong to open a bank account there. I need to enter the new-site contests every month and keep looking for new keywords, so I can run faster and grow faster.

Third, keep the input and the output going. Input is continuous learning, online and off, and it also includes reading, especially paper books. I have slowly been forming this habit lately, and I need to keep it up. Output matters more, of course. Whether it is a personal blog, a WeChat official account, or talks in person, I have to push myself to write and to speak, and let output force the input along.

Last, I will gradually build up a personal knowledge base. The faster AI develops, the more I need a complete, clear personal context, and a knowledge base is the best vessel for it. I will keep learning WeKnora thoroughly, this open-source project, and use it well, putting into it everything worth keeping.

That is all for now. It is hard to imagine what things will look like in three years, or in five. I will just start running, and stay optimistic.
