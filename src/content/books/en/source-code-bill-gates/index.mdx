> I read this book alongside *Finite and Infinite Games*, mainly because that book was so obscure that reading a personal biography felt much easier. But the writing here is poor, not as good as *Steve Jobs*, and because it's an autobiography, it inevitably glosses over faults and tells only the parts that flatter him.

## 1. Overview

Bill Gates is an influential, change-leading business leader and philanthropist of our time. This book tells the story of his growing up.

Gates's brilliant business achievements are well known. At 20 he left Harvard and founded a software company that would become an industry giant, changing the way the whole world works and lives. The billionaire then turned his attention to philanthropy, taking on the challenges of climate change, global health, and education in the United States.

This book is not Gates reminiscing about Microsoft's glory days, nor is it about the birth of the Gates Foundation or the future of technology. It is a warm, human personal story, in which you'll see how Bill Gates became who he is today and learn about the passions and ambitions of his childhood and teenage years.

In this story appear his self-disciplined grandmother and ambitious parents, his first deep friendships and the sudden death of a close friend, his struggle to fit in with the society around him, his wondrous discovery of code and computers as a new era was dawning, and his search for his path in life that began in his youth. On that path, he once snuck out of the house in the middle of the night to a nearby computer center; later, in his college dorm, he lit the fuse of a technological revolution that would change the world.

In this book, for the first time, Bill Gates tells his story candidly, with wisdom and warmth, vividly presenting his remarkable life to readers.

## 3. Summary

Bill Gates is a smart guy who figured out early how to make money from software. That idea, spread far and wide, is what lets programmers like me earn a living today.

Recently I also saw people discussing this book on X. One interesting view was roughly that Gates succeeded because of his lawyer father and his mother, an executive at IBM. I do envy Gates's family and the good education he got. But I admire even more his love of reading, his refusal to limit himself, and his ability to keep breaking through his own limits. That's what we should learn from!
